# Møller–Plesset Perturbation Theory

This document provides a clear, simple, and mathematically rigorous explanation of Møller–Plesset perturbation theory in second quantization. Building directly upon the Hartree–Fock ground state and canonical Fock operator, this guide explains how the electronic Hamiltonian is partitioned, how Rayleigh–Schrödinger perturbation theory evaluates electron correlation, and how the second-order (MP2) energy correction is derived in the spin-orbital basis.

---

## I. Hamiltonian Partitioning and Perturbation Theory

### 1. Møller–Plesset Partitioning and Unperturbed Eigenstates

The Hartree–Fock method provides an optimal single-determinant reference state $|\Phi_0\rangle$ and canonical orbital energies $\epsilon_p$, but neglects the instantaneous Coulomb repulsion between electrons. This missing interaction is called electron correlation, and the correlation energy is defined as the difference between the exact non-relativistic electronic energy and the Hartree–Fock energy, $E_{\text{corr}}=E_{\text{exact}}-E_{\text{HF}}$. Møller–Plesset perturbation theory calculates this correlation energy by partitioning the exact electronic Hamiltonian $\hat{H}$ into an unperturbed part $\hat{H}_0$ and a perturbation $\hat{V}$ called the fluctuation potential as

$$
\hat{H}=\hat{H}_0+\hat{V}
$$

The unperturbed Hamiltonian $\hat{H}_0$ is chosen as the sum of the effective one-electron canonical Fock operators from Hartree–Fock, written in second quantization as

$$
\hat{H}_0=\sum_p\epsilon_p a_p^\dagger a_p
$$

where $p$ runs over all canonical spin-orbitals with orbital energies $\epsilon_p$. The Hartree–Fock reference determinant $|\Phi_0\rangle$ is an exact eigenstate of $\hat{H}_0$, with its unperturbed zeroth-order energy given by the sum of the occupied spin-orbital energies as

$$
\hat{H}_0|\Phi_0\rangle=E_0^{(0)}|\Phi_0\rangle=\left(\sum_i^{\text{occ}}\epsilon_i\right)|\Phi_0\rangle
$$

Excited Slater determinants are generated by applying particle creation operators $a_a^\dagger,a_b^\dagger,\dots$ and hole annihilation operators $a_i,a_j,\dots$ to the reference state $|\Phi_0\rangle$. Evaluating the commutators with the unperturbed Hamiltonian using canonical anticommutation relations gives $[\hat{H}_0,a_a^\dagger]=\epsilon_a a_a^\dagger$ and $[\hat{H}_0,a_i]=-\epsilon_i a_i$. Consequently, any excited determinant $|\Phi_{i_1\cdots i_n}^{a_1\cdots a_n}\rangle=a_{a_1}^\dagger\cdots a_{a_n}^\dagger a_{i_n}\cdots a_{i_1}|\Phi_0\rangle$ is also an exact eigenstate of $\hat{H}_0$ as

$$
\hat{H}_0|\Phi_{i_1\cdots i_n}^{a_1\cdots a_n}\rangle=\left(E_0^{(0)}+\sum_{\alpha=1}^n\epsilon_{a_\alpha}-\sum_{\alpha=1}^n\epsilon_{i_\alpha}\right)|\Phi_{i_1\cdots i_n}^{a_1\cdots a_n}\rangle
$$

The unperturbed excitation energy relative to the reference ground state therefore simplifies to a sum of single-particle orbital energy differences, expressed as

$$
E_0^{(0)}-E_{i_1\cdots i_n}^{a_1\cdots a_n(0)}=\sum_{\alpha=1}^n\epsilon_{i_\alpha}-\sum_{\alpha=1}^n\epsilon_{a_\alpha}
$$

which forms the characteristic energy denominators appearing in the perturbation expansion.

### 2. Rayleigh–Schrödinger Expansion and Low-Order Energies

To determine the correlation energy systematically, we introduce a continuous coupling parameter $\lambda\in[0,1]$ that interpolates between the unperturbed system and the physical Hamiltonian as $\hat{H}(\lambda)=\hat{H}_0+\lambda\hat{V}$. We expand the exact electronic state $|\Psi\rangle$ and energy $E$ as Taylor series in powers of $\lambda$ as

$$
|\Psi\rangle=\sum_{m=0}^\infty\lambda^m|\Psi^{(m)}\rangle,\quad E=\sum_{m=0}^\infty\lambda^m E^{(m)}
$$

where $|\Psi^{(0)}\rangle=|\Phi_0\rangle$. We adopt the intermediate normalization condition $\langle\Phi_0|\Psi\rangle=1$, which enforces orthogonality between the reference state and all higher-order wavefunction corrections, $\langle\Phi_0|\Psi^{(m)}\rangle=\delta_{m0}$.

Inserting these expansions into the Schrödinger equation $(\hat{H}_0+\lambda\hat{V})|\Psi\rangle=E|\Psi\rangle$ and collecting terms order by order in $\lambda$ yields

$$
(\hat{H}_0-E_0^{(0)})|\Psi^{(m)}\rangle+(\hat{V}-E^{(1)})|\Psi^{(m-1)}\rangle=\sum_{j=2}^m E^{(j)}|\Psi^{(m-j)}\rangle
$$

Projecting this equation from the left with $\langle\Phi_0|$ eliminates the first term because $\langle\Phi_0|(\hat{H}_0-E_0^{(0)})=0$. Applying intermediate normalization eliminates the right-hand sum for all $m\ge1$, giving the general expression for the energy correction at order $m$ as

$$
E^{(m)}=\langle\Phi_0|\hat{V}|\Psi^{(m-1)}\rangle
$$

At zeroth order, the energy is the reference expectation value $E^{(0)}=\sum_i^{\text{occ}}\epsilon_i$. At first order, the energy correction is the expectation value of the fluctuation potential $\hat{V}=\hat{H}-\hat{H}_0$ over the reference state, evaluating to

$$
E^{(1)}=\langle\Phi_0|\hat{V}|\Phi_0\rangle=\langle\Phi_0|(\hat{H}-\hat{H}_0)|\Phi_0\rangle=E_{\text{HF}}-E_0^{(0)}
$$

Summing the zeroth- and first-order energies recovers the Hartree–Fock energy exactly as

$$
E^{(0)}+E^{(1)}=E_0^{(0)}+\left(E_{\text{HF}}-E_0^{(0)}\right)=E_{\text{HF}}
$$

This fundamental result demonstrates that the Møller–Plesset partitioning already accounts for the full Hartree–Fock mean-field energy through first order. Electron correlation effects therefore begin strictly at second order as $E_{\text{corr}}=\sum_{m=2}^\infty E^{(m)}$.

## II. Second-Order Correlation Energy (MP2)

### 3. First-Order Wavefunction and Excitation Amplitudes

To evaluate the second-order correlation energy $E^{(2)}=\langle\Phi_0|\hat{V}|\Psi^{(1)}\rangle$, we project the first-order perturbation equation $(\hat{H}_0-E_0^{(0)})|\Psi^{(1)}\rangle+(\hat{V}-E^{(1)})|\Phi_0\rangle=0$ onto an excited determinant $\langle\Phi_n|$. Because $\langle\Phi_n|\Phi_0\rangle=0$ and $\hat{H}_0|\Phi_n\rangle=E_n^{(0)}|\Phi_n\rangle$, the configuration mixing amplitude isolates as

$$
c_n^{(1)}=\langle\Phi_n|\Psi^{(1)}\rangle=\frac{\langle\Phi_n|\hat{V}|\Phi_0\rangle}{E_0^{(0)}-E_n^{(0)}}=\frac{\langle\Phi_n|\hat{H}|\Phi_0\rangle}{E_0^{(0)}-E_n^{(0)}}
$$

where the last equality follows from $\langle\Phi_n|\hat{H}_0|\Phi_0\rangle=E_0^{(0)}\langle\Phi_n|\Phi_0\rangle=0$. We evaluate this numerator across all excitation classes.

For singly excited determinants $|\Phi_i^a\rangle=a_a^\dagger a_i|\Phi_0\rangle$, Brillouin's theorem established in Hartree–Fock theory guarantees that the Hamiltonian does not couple the reference state to any single excitation, $\langle\Phi_i^a|\hat{H}|\Phi_0\rangle=0$. Consequently, single excitations vanish from the first-order wavefunction as

$$
c_i^{a(1)}=0
$$

For doubly excited determinants $|\Phi_{ij}^{ab}\rangle=a_a^\dagger a_b^\dagger a_j a_i|\Phi_0\rangle$, evaluating the matrix element of the two-body Hamiltonian $\frac{1}{2}\sum_{pqrs}g_{pqrs}a_p^\dagger a_q^\dagger a_s a_r$ using Wick's theorem pairs the creation and annihilation operators to yield the antisymmetrized two-electron integral as

$$
\langle\Phi_{ij}^{ab}|\hat{H}|\Phi_0\rangle=\langle ij||ab\rangle=g_{ijab}-g_{ijba}=\langle ij|ab\rangle-\langle ij|ba\rangle
$$

The corresponding first-order double-excitation amplitudes are therefore given by

$$
c_{ij}^{ab(1)}=\frac{\langle ij||ab\rangle}{\epsilon_i+\epsilon_j-\epsilon_a-\epsilon_b}
$$

For triply and higher excited determinants ($n\ge3$), the matrix elements $\langle\Phi_n|\hat{H}|\Phi_0\rangle$ vanish identically because the Hamiltonian contains at most two-body operators. The first-order wavefunction is therefore composed purely of doubly excited configurations as

$$
|\Psi^{(1)}\rangle=\sum_{i<j}^{\text{occ}}\sum_{a<b}^{\text{virt}}\frac{\langle ij||ab\rangle}{\epsilon_i+\epsilon_j-\epsilon_a-\epsilon_b}|\Phi_{ij}^{ab}\rangle
$$

### 4. Spin-Orbital MP2 Energy Formula

Inserting the first-order wavefunction into the second-order energy expression $E^{(2)}=\langle\Phi_0|\hat{V}|\Psi^{(1)}\rangle$ yields the restricted sum

$$
E^{(2)}=\sum_{i<j}^{\text{occ}}\sum_{a<b}^{\text{virt}}\langle\Phi_0|\hat{H}|\Phi_{ij}^{ab}\rangle c_{ij}^{ab(1)}=\sum_{i<j}^{\text{occ}}\sum_{a<b}^{\text{virt}}\frac{|\langle ij||ab\rangle|^2}{\epsilon_i+\epsilon_j-\epsilon_a-\epsilon_b}
$$

The index restrictions $i<j$ and $a<b$ ensure each physical pair excitation is counted once. Because the numerator $|\langle ij||ab\rangle|^2$ and denominator $\epsilon_i+\epsilon_j-\epsilon_a-\epsilon_b$ are invariant under swapping $i\leftrightarrow j$ or $a\leftrightarrow b$, and vanish when $i=j$ or $a=b$ by the Pauli exclusion principle, we convert the restricted sum into an unrestricted sum over all occupied and virtual spin-orbitals by introducing a prefactor of $1/4$ as

$$
E^{(2)}=\frac{1}{4}\sum_{i,j}^{\text{occ}}\sum_{a,b}^{\text{virt}}\frac{|\langle ij||ab\rangle|^2}{\epsilon_i+\epsilon_j-\epsilon_a-\epsilon_b}
$$

In a stable Hartree–Fock reference, the orbital energies of occupied states are strictly lower than those of virtual states ($\epsilon_i+\epsilon_j<\epsilon_a+\epsilon_b$). Every energy denominator in the summation is therefore negative, guaranteeing that the second-order correlation energy correction is strictly non-positive, $E^{(2)}\le0$, lowering the electronic energy below the Hartree–Fock limit.
