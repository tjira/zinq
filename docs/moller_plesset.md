# Møller–Plesset Perturbation Theory

This document provides a clear, simple, and mathematically rigorous explanation of Møller–Plesset perturbation theory in second quantization and determinant space. Building directly upon the Hartree–Fock ground state and canonical Fock operator, this guide explains how the electronic Hamiltonian is partitioned, how Rayleigh–Schrödinger perturbation theory evaluates electron correlation, and how the perturbation series is solved recursively in the Slater determinant basis.

---

## I. Hamiltonian Partitioning and Perturbation Theory

### 1. Møller–Plesset Partitioning and Unperturbed Eigenstates

The Hartree–Fock method provides an optimal single-determinant reference state $|\Phi_0\rangle$ and canonical orbital energies $\epsilon_p$, but neglects instantaneous Coulomb repulsion between electrons. This missing interaction is called electron correlation, and the correlation energy is defined as the difference between the exact non-relativistic electronic energy and the Hartree–Fock energy, $E_{\text{corr}}=E_{\text{exact}}-E_{\text{HF}}$. Møller–Plesset perturbation theory calculates this correlation energy by partitioning the exact electronic Hamiltonian $\hat{H}$ into an unperturbed part $\hat{H}_0$ and a perturbation $\hat{V}$ called the fluctuation potential as

$$
\hat{H}=\hat{H}_0+\hat{V}
$$

The unperturbed Hamiltonian $\hat{H}_0$ is chosen as the sum of the effective one-electron canonical Fock operators from Hartree–Fock, written in second quantization as

$$
\hat{H}_0=\sum_p\epsilon_p a_p^\dagger a_p
$$

where $p$ runs over all canonical spin-orbitals with orbital energies $\epsilon_p$. The Hartree–Fock reference determinant $|\Phi_0\rangle$ is an exact eigenstate of $\hat{H}_0$, with its unperturbed zeroth-order energy given by the sum of the occupied spin-orbital energies as

$$
\hat{H}_0|\Phi_0\rangle=E_0^{(0)}|\Phi_0\rangle=\left(\sum_i^{\text{occ}}\epsilon_i\right)|\Phi_0\rangle
$$

Excited Slater determinants are generated by applying particle creation operators $a_a^\dagger,a_b^\dagger,\dots$ and hole annihilation operators $a_i,a_j,\dots$ to the reference state $|\Phi_0\rangle$. Evaluating the commutators with the unperturbed Hamiltonian using canonical anticommutation relations gives $[\hat{H}_0,a_a^\dagger]=\epsilon_a a_a^\dagger$ and $[\hat{H}_0,a_i]=-\epsilon_i a_i$. Consequently, any excited determinant $|\Phi_{i_1\cdots i_n}^{a_1\cdots a_n}\rangle=a_{a_1}^\dagger\cdots a_{a_n}^\dagger a_{i_n}\cdots a_{i_1}|\Phi_0\rangle$ is also an exact eigenstate of $\hat{H}_0$ as

$$
\hat{H}_0|\Phi_{i_1\cdots i_n}^{a_1\cdots a_n}\rangle=\left(E_0^{(0)}+\sum_{\alpha=1}^n\epsilon_{a_\alpha}-\sum_{\alpha=1}^n\epsilon_{i_\alpha}\right)|\Phi_{i_1\cdots i_n}^{a_1\cdots a_n}\rangle
$$

The unperturbed excitation energy relative to the reference ground state therefore simplifies to a sum of single-particle orbital energy differences, expressed as

$$
E_0^{(0)}-E_{i_1\cdots i_n}^{a_1\cdots a_n(0)}=\sum_{\alpha=1}^n\epsilon_{i_\alpha}-\sum_{\alpha=1}^n\epsilon_{a_\alpha}
$$

which forms the characteristic energy denominators appearing in the perturbation expansion.

### 2. Rayleigh–Schrödinger Expansion and Low-Order Energies

To determine the correlation energy systematically, we introduce a continuous coupling parameter $\lambda\in[0,1]$ that interpolates between the unperturbed system and the physical Hamiltonian as $\hat{H}(\lambda)=\hat{H}_0+\lambda\hat{V}$. We expand the exact electronic state $|\Psi\rangle$ and energy $E$ as Taylor series in powers of $\lambda$ as

$$
|\Psi\rangle=\sum_{m=0}^\infty\lambda^m|\Psi^{(m)}\rangle,\quad E=\sum_{m=0}^\infty\lambda^m E^{(m)}
$$

where $|\Psi^{(0)}\rangle=|\Phi_0\rangle$. We adopt the intermediate normalization condition $\langle\Phi_0|\Psi\rangle=1$, which enforces orthogonality between the reference state and all higher-order wavefunction corrections, $\langle\Phi_0|\Psi^{(m)}\rangle=\delta_{m0}$.

Inserting these expansions into the Schrödinger equation $(\hat{H}_0+\lambda\hat{V})|\Psi\rangle=E|\Psi\rangle$ and collecting terms order by order in $\lambda$ yields

$$
(\hat{H}_0-E_0^{(0)})|\Psi^{(m)}\rangle+(\hat{V}-E^{(1)})|\Psi^{(m-1)}\rangle=\sum_{j=2}^m E^{(j)}|\Psi^{(m-j)}\rangle
$$

Projecting this equation from the left with $\langle\Phi_0|$ eliminates the first term because $\langle\Phi_0|(\hat{H}_0-E_0^{(0)})=0$. Applying intermediate normalization eliminates the right-hand sum for all $m\ge1$, giving the general expression for the energy correction at order $m$ as

$$
E^{(m)}=\langle\Phi_0|\hat{V}|\Psi^{(m-1)}\rangle
$$

At zeroth order, the energy is the reference expectation value $E^{(0)}=\sum_i^{\text{occ}}\epsilon_i$. At first order, the energy correction is the expectation value of the fluctuation potential $\hat{V}=\hat{H}-\hat{H}_0$ over the reference state, evaluating to

$$
E^{(1)}=\langle\Phi_0|\hat{V}|\Phi_0\rangle=\langle\Phi_0|(\hat{H}-\hat{H}_0)|\Phi_0\rangle=E_{\text{HF}}-E_0^{(0)}
$$

Summing the zeroth- and first-order energies recovers the Hartree–Fock energy exactly as

$$
E^{(0)}+E^{(1)}=E_0^{(0)}+\left(E_{\text{HF}}-E_0^{(0)}\right)=E_{\text{HF}}
$$

This fundamental result demonstrates that the Møller–Plesset partitioning already accounts for the full Hartree–Fock mean-field energy through first order. Electron correlation effects therefore begin strictly at second order as $E_{\text{corr}}=\sum_{m=2}^\infty E^{(m)}$.

## II. Determinant-Space Representation and Slater–Condon Evaluation

### 3. Configuration Expansion and Projected Recurrence

To evaluate the perturbation series for arbitrary orders, the wavefunctions $|\Psi^{(m)}\rangle$ are expanded in the complete orthonormal basis of Slater determinants $\{|\Phi_n\rangle\}$, where $|\Phi_0\rangle$ is the Hartree–Fock reference and $|\Phi_n\rangle$ for $n\ge1$ are excited configurations generated by particle-hole excitations. Intermediate normalization requires the reference coefficient to satisfy $c_0^{(0)}=1$ and $c_0^{(m)}=0$ for all $m\ge1$. Projecting the $m$-th order perturbation equation onto an excited determinant $\langle\Phi_i|$ ($i\ge1$) and using the unperturbed eigenvalue relation $\hat{H}_0|\Phi_i\rangle=E_i^{(0)}|\Phi_i\rangle$ yields

$$
(E_i^{(0)}-E_0^{(0)})c_i^{(m)}+\langle\Phi_i|\hat{V}|\Psi^{(m-1)}\rangle-E^{(1)}c_i^{(m-1)}=\sum_{j=2}^m E^{(j)}c_i^{(m-j)}
$$

Substituting the fluctuation potential $\hat{V}=\hat{H}-\hat{H}_0$ and noting that $\langle\Phi_i|\hat{H}_0|\Psi^{(m-1)}\rangle=E_i^{(0)}c_i^{(m-1)}$, the matrix elements of $\hat{V}$ simplify to $\langle\Phi_i|\hat{V}|\Psi^{(m-1)}\rangle=\sum_j H_{ij}c_j^{(m-1)}-E_i^{(0)}c_i^{(m-1)}$, where $H_{ij}=\langle\Phi_i|\hat{H}|\Phi_j\rangle$ are matrix elements of the physical electronic Hamiltonian $\hat{H}$. Rearranging terms isolates the configuration mixing amplitudes at order $m$ as

$$
c_i^{(m)}=\frac{\sum_j H_{ij}c_j^{(m-1)}-E_i^{(0)}c_i^{(m-1)}-\sum_{j=1}^m E^{(j)}c_i^{(m-j)}}{E_0^{(0)}-E_i^{(0)}}
$$

Because the reference determinant satisfies $\langle\Phi_0|\hat{H}_0|\Phi_j\rangle=0$ for all orthogonal excited determinants $j\ge1$, the perturbation matrix element simplifies to $\langle\Phi_0|\hat{V}|\Phi_j\rangle=\langle\Phi_0|\hat{H}|\Phi_j\rangle=H_{0j}$, giving the general energy correction at order $m$ as

$$
E^{(m)}=\sum_{j=1}^{N_{\text{dets}}}H_{0j}c_j^{(m-1)}
$$

which evaluates the exact correlation correction at any perturbation order from the Hamiltonian matrix and previous-order expansion coefficients.

### 4. Matrix Elements via Slater–Condon Rules and Correlation Orders

As derived in Configuration Interaction theory, the Hamiltonian matrix elements $H_{ij}=\langle\Phi_i|\hat{H}|\Phi_j\rangle$ are evaluated between determinant pairs using the Slater–Condon rules over the one-electron core Hamiltonian $\mathbf{h}$ and two-electron repulsion tensor $\mathbf{g}$. From the canonical Hartree–Fock reference, the core Hamiltonian elements are constructed from the orbital energies $\epsilon_p$ and antisymmetrized repulsion integrals as

$$
h_{pq}=\epsilon_p\delta_{pq}-\sum_i^{\text{occ}}\left(g_{piqi}-g_{piiq}\right)
$$

Applying the Hamiltonian subspace block structure and Brillouin's theorem established in Configuration Interaction theory, the reference state decouples from all single excitations ($H_{0,\Phi_i^a}=0$) and couples directly only to double excitations ($H_{0,\Phi_{ij}^{ab}}=\langle ij||ab\rangle=g_{ijab}-g_{ijba}$), while matrix elements with triply and higher excited determinants vanish identically because the Hamiltonian contains at most two-body operators.

At second order ($m=2$), the correlation energy evaluates to $E^{(2)}=\sum_{j\neq0}H_{0j}c_j^{(1)}$ with first-order amplitudes $c_j^{(1)}=H_{j0}/(E_0^{(0)}-E_j^{(0)})$, recovering the physical pair correlation energy. For higher orders such as MP3 ($m=3$) and MP4 ($m=4$), Hamiltonian couplings between singles, doubles, triples, and quadruples determinants recursively generate the complete correlation energy series without truncation errors.
